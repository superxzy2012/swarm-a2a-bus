# noon Cross-Border Selling for a China-Incorporated Company — Hard Fact-Check

Research date: 2026-10-30. Every claim below carries its source URL and the publication / last-updated date shown on that source page. Where a page shows no date, that is stated. Items no retrievable source could answer are marked **UNKNOWN**. Nothing is inferred from "typical industry practice."

**Key structural finding that frames all 5 answers:** noon runs two different things that are easily confused:
- **(A) noon Global Shipping (NGS)** — a China-specific cross-border programme. The seller stays in China, drops orders at noon's Shenzhen consolidation hub, noon handles international shipping, customs and destination VAT, and the seller is paid a self-set USD "Transfer Price."
- **(B) The standard "International Seller" marketplace route** — a foreign company registers on noon UAE/KSA as an international seller and fulfils via FBN/FBP in the destination market. noon's own pages impose materially different (and internally inconsistent) VAT/document requirements on this route.
- **Egypt:** neither route is open to a Chinese company (see Q1/Q5).

---

## 1. Can a Chinese-incorporated company open a noon cross-border store directly (without a local company)? Required qualification documents

**Answer: YES — for UAE and KSA (and, as destination markets listed by noon, Bahrain, Qatar, Kuwait and Oman), via noon Global Shipping (NGS). NO for Egypt.**

noon's NGS page states: "sellers based in China can directly reach customers in the Middle East (UAE, KSA, Bahrain, Qatar, Kuwait, and Oman) without having to set up their business locally. (i.e. no need to have a UAE or KSA Trade license and VAT)" and "if you are a seller registered in China with a valid Chinese Commercial Registration you are eligible to list and sell under noon Global Shipping model." Onboarding creates one store for UAE and one store for KSA; the seller creates a warehouse at a local address in China (used only to update inventory levels, not for shipping) and drops orders at noon's consolidation hub in Shenzhen, China.

**Documents/requirements the NGS (China) page itself names, item by item, in the source's exact English wording:**
1. **"valid Chinese Commercial Registration"** — eligibility requirement.
2. **"Company Registration document"** — the document uploaded when creating the Legal Entity, together with the business's "License number, Registered Name and Address, Issue Date and Expiry Date."
3. Tax details step: select **"Non VAT Registered"** (a selection in Seller Lab, not a document — noon does not ask the China NGS seller for a UAE/KSA VAT certificate on this page).
4. An **authorized signatory** must be added, who receives and signs the programme T&Cs (the page does **not** name any signatory ID document for the China route).
5. A **valid payment method** — "A valid payment method is required for new Chinese sellers before taking their store live"; payout options named: Bank Account, Payoneer, LianLian, World First, Ping Pong.

**UNKNOWN within Q1:** noon's China NGS page does **not** publish a complete qualification-document checklist comparable to the one it publishes for Indian Global sellers (which lists five named documents). Whether a passport/ID, bank proof or other documents are additionally demanded during Seller Lab Legal Entity approval for a Chinese company cannot be confirmed from any retrievable public noon page — the full checklist, if it exists, is behind the seller registration flow / account-manager onboarding. A secondary source (LianLian Global, a noon payout partner, in Chinese) lists the preparation materials for Chinese sellers as: 营业执照 (business licence), 法人护照 (legal representative's passport), 收款账户 (payout/receiving account), and says onboarding is via a noon account manager ("招商经理1V1") — labelled secondary, and its document names are Chinese, not noon's English names.

**Contrast — the standard "International Seller" route (not NGS).** noon's "Documents Required to Sell on noon" page lists, for International Sellers, in its exact English wording:
- **"Trade License"** — "It must have a validity of at least 30 days"; for UAE: "Company registered anywhere in the world except UAE"; for KSA: "Company registered anywhere in the world except KSA"; the licence "must authorize trading, selling, or manufacturing activities… licenses limited to service provision are not accepted."
- **"VAT Registration Certificate"** — "VAT Registration Certificate issued in the UAE" / "VAT Registration Certificate issued in the KSA."
- **"Valid Identity Proof"** — "You need to submit your Passport as an identification proof." (The ID must be of the person named on the trade licence, otherwise a power of attorney is required.)
- **"Bank Account Details"** — "Details of your Payoneer or Lian Lian account in the form of an authorization letter, duly signed and stamped with your company seal."
- **Egypt column for every document type: "Not applicable: Your company has to be mandatorily registered in Egypt to be able sell in noon Egypt."**

⚠️ **Disagreement flagged (inside noon's own pages):** the documents page includes the destination-country "VAT Registration Certificate" in the International Seller table, and noon's VAT FAQ states it outright — "If you are an International Seller i.e. if your company is not registered in UAE, KSA or Egypt VAT registration certificate issued by the country in which you wish to sell is mandatory" — while the NGS China page says no UAE/KSA VAT is needed at all. Two secondary guides (Blooprint; ecomsellertool) read the documents-page table as saying the VAT certificate is *not* required for international sellers, i.e. even the reading of that table is disputed. The safe statement is: **NGS route = no local VAT certificate per the NGS page; standard International Seller route = noon's VAT FAQ says a destination-country VAT certificate is mandatory.** These are different routes; do not mix them.

## 3. Logistics options and fees — FBN, seller self-shipping / cross-border shipping, and cross-border delivery times

**Answer — the cross-border (NGS) option first, because it is the one a China seller actually uses:**
- Model: seller packs the order (no noon packaging material required) and ships or drops it at noon's consolidation hub in Shenzhen, China, within the processing time the seller set for the China warehouse. From the hub, "noon will take care of international shipping, customs clearance, and last-mile delivery to the customer."
- **Fee charged to the seller for the cross-border leg: none stated as a price.** noon's NGS page says the Transfer Price "should exclude all international logistics-related costs such as cross-border shipping, customs duties, taxes, and clearance fees, as these are fully handled by noon," and "noon will cover the costs related to international logistics, customs duties, clearance, and other cross-border operational expenses and will be charged to the customer directly." **The amount charged to the customer is not stated on the page — UNKNOWN.** The seller's own cost of getting the parcel from its premises to the Shenzhen hub is not priced by noon (secondary source 10100.com says the domestic China leg is at the seller's own expense — secondary only).
- Returns: not shipped back to China by noon; saleable returns are listed as local FBN inventory (FBN rate card then applies; FBN storage fee waived for up to 60 days); non-saleable returns are liquidated in UAE/KSA with no inventory removal/disposal fee and no proceeds to the seller, or returned to the seller's local warehouse (RTV) at the seller's choice.

**FBN (Fulfilled by noon) fees — for in-market stock (UAE/KSA/Egypt local or international-route sellers, and NGS returns resold as FBN). All fees exclusive of VAT, per unit:**
- **UAE outbound fee** (from 10 Sep 2026, by size tier/weight and sale price ASP ≤25 AED / >25 AED): Small envelope 5.5 / 7.5 AED; Standard envelope ≤0.1 kg 6 / 8 AED up to ≤0.5 kg 6.5 / 8.5 AED; Large envelope 7 / 9 AED; Standard parcel ≤0.25 kg 7.2 / 9.2 AED rising to >2–3 kg 10.5 / 12.5 AED, +1 AED per additional kg to 12 kg; Oversize parcel ≤1 kg 10.5 / 12.5 AED rising to >25–30 kg 39.5 / 41.5 AED, +1 AED/kg above 30 kg. **Monthly storage: 1.75 AED per cubic foot per month (all categories, from 1 Oct 2026)**; long-term storage (>365 days) 25 AED/CBF; non-saleable storage (>30 days) 12 AED/CBF; return administration fee: lesser of 15 AED or 20% of the referral fee.
- **KSA outbound fee** (from 10 Sep 2026, ASP <25 SAR / >25 SAR): Small envelope 5.5 / 7.5 SAR; Standard parcel ≤0.25 kg 7.2 / 9.2 SAR rising to >2–3 kg 10 / 13 SAR, +1 SAR/kg to 12 kg; Oversize ≤1 kg 10 / 14 SAR rising to >25–30 kg 39 / 43 SAR. **Monthly storage: 2.75 SAR/CBF/month**; long-term 25 SAR/CBF; return administration fee: lesser of 15 SAR or 20% of referral fee.
- **Egypt outbound fee** (from 1 Feb 2026): Small/Standard envelope 19 EGP; Standard parcel ≤0.25 kg 19 EGP rising to >2–3 kg 23 EGP; Oversize ≤1 kg 24.5 EGP rising to >25–30 kg 82.5 EGP; Bulky ≤20 kg 74.5 EGP. (Egypt FBN page lists no monthly storage rate in the fetched text; long-term storage >180 days is 35 EGP/CBF.)

**Seller self-shipping within the destination market (FBP "Directship" — inventory at seller's own warehouse in UAE/KSA, noon collects and delivers; this is NOT China-to-Middle-East shipping):**
- **UAE Directship Outbound Fee** (per shipment, higher of physical/volumetric weight): Standard Parcel ≤0.25 kg — pickup 14.0 AED / drop-off 12.0 AED; ≤0.5 kg 14.5 / 12.5; ≤1 kg 15.0 / 13.0; ≤3 kg 17.5 / 15.5; +1 AED/kg to 12 kg; Oversize ≤1 kg 18.5 / 16.5; >25–30 kg 44.0 / 42.0 AED. Sellers are reimbursed the customer shipping fee up to a maximum of 10 AED per order (customer shipping charge: 10 AED if order <100 AED; 5 AED if 100–500 AED; 0 if >500 AED).
- **KSA Directship Outbound Fee**: Standard Parcel ≤0.25 kg — pickup 19.0 SAR / drop-off 17.0 SAR; ≤0.5 kg 19.5 / 17.5; ≤1 kg 20.5 / 18.5; >2–3 kg 29.0 / 27.0; Oversize ≤1 kg 28.5 / 26.5; >25–30 kg 53.0 / 51.0 SAR. Reimbursement up to a maximum of 12 SAR per order (customer charge 12 SAR <100 SAR; 6 SAR 100–500 SAR; 0 >500 SAR).

**Delivery times for cross-border (China → customer) orders:**
- **noon's own NGS page states NO delivery time in days — UNKNOWN from primary sources.** It only defines the seller-side "Processing time: Time that will take for you to fulfil the order and drop it off at the nearest noon's consolidation hub in China."
- ⚠️ **Secondary sources disagree with each other (flagged, not averaged):** LianLian Global says the noon Global mode via the Shenzhen transit warehouse takes **6–10 days**; 大数跨境 (10100.com) says NGS overall fulfilment is **7–15 days** (air freight 3–7 days + clearance and last-mile 2–8 days), versus 1–3 days for FBN. Neither figure appears on any noon page found.

## 4. Payout / settlement — cycle and currency for cross-border sellers

**Answer:**
- **NGS trigger:** "noon pays the seller once an item is successfully delivered to the customer," and "Payouts follow noon's regular Payment Processing Policy." Payout amount = the seller's Transfer Price, with no fees deducted (NGS page).
- **Cycle (regular Payment Processing Policy, which NGS incorporates):** statements are generated **every Wednesday**; for **UAE & KSA, payouts are processed on Thursday of the following week** after the Wednesday statement (Egypt: Tuesday of the following week); "Payments will appear in your bank account within 1 to 2 business days from the due date." So the effective lag from a Wednesday statement to processing is ~8 days, plus 1–2 business days to appear.
- **Minimum threshold for international-currency accounts:** accumulated earnings must reach **1,000 AED / SAR** (marketplace UAE/KSA) before a weekly payout is released; below-threshold balances roll over to the next cycle. (Local-currency accounts: 100 AED/SAR/EGP.) Note: an older-format noon payments page on the supportagent portal states different thresholds (AED/SAR 1,000 for all UAE/KSA weekly payments, monthly processing for 50–1,000) — ⚠️ **flagged disagreement between noon's own two payments pages**; the helpcenter "Payments processing policy" quoted here is the one updated 2026-10-30.
- **Currency:** For NGS, the seller sets the **Transfer Price in USD** ("Add in your transfer price for each product in USD"; the price the seller uploads is "the price in USD of a product (called Transfer Price)"). The NGS page does **not** contain an explicit sentence "settlement currency is USD." The explicit statement exists on noon's parallel Global Selling guide for Indian sellers: "Q. What is the currency of the payment settlement? noon settles all payments in the USD," with the bank account required to be "compatible with receiving payments in USD." That guide is for India, not China — cited as the closest primary analogue, **not** as proof for China. Payout channels named for Chinese/international sellers on the NGS page: Bank Account, Payoneer, LianLian, World First, Ping Pong.
- ⚠️ Secondary source 10100.com additionally claims a 7–14 day funds-freeze after delivery before settlement and 3–5 business days for third-party payout tools; **no such freeze period appears in the noon Payment Processing Policy or NGS pages fetched — unverified.**

## 5. Local VAT / tax registration — UAE, Saudi Arabia, Egypt

**Answer, by market and by route (the route distinction is decisive):**

- **UAE & KSA via NGS (the China cross-border programme): NO local VAT registration is required, according to noon's NGS page.** The seller creates the Legal Entity selecting "Non VAT Registered." noon states: "Who is responsible for VAT in destination countries? noon calculates and pays the applicable VAT to the respective tax authorities based on the prevailing tax rates in each destination market. Sellers are not required to register for VAT or manage VAT payments in the destination countries." The same position appears in noon's Global Selling guide (Indian lane): "No, VAT registration is not mandatory for noon Global Stores in either the UAE or KSA," and "noon collects the applicable VAT in both the UAE and KSA and pays it directly to the relevant authorities. VAT or other import-related charges will not appear on the seller's invoice. Only the VAT applicable to Marketplace Fees (UAE: 5%, KSA: 15%) will be reflected in the seller's invoice" — i.e. VAT on noon's own fees still applies to the seller even on the cross-border programme.
- **UAE & KSA via the standard International Seller route: noon's pages conflict, but the explicit FAQ answer is that registration IS required.** noon's VAT FAQ: "If you are an International Seller i.e. if your company is not registered in UAE, KSA or Egypt VAT registration certificate issued by the country in which you wish to sell is mandatory," and the documents page lists a "VAT Registration Certificate issued in the UAE" / "issued in the KSA" for international sellers. ⚠️ As flagged in Q1, two secondary guides read the documents page the other way; the contradiction is in noon's own material and is **not resolved by any primary source found**. A Chinese seller must therefore confirm with noon in writing which route it is being onboarded onto — NGS (non-VAT) or International Seller (VAT certificate mandatory per the FAQ).
- **Egypt: the question does not arise.** noon's documents page states for every document type that a company "has to be mandatorily registered in Egypt to be able sell in noon Egypt," and Egypt is not among the NGS destination markets noon lists (UAE, KSA, Bahrain, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman). **No noon cross-border route for a Chinese company into Egypt was found in any source.**
- **Handling, per sources:** under NGS, Chinese sellers handle destination VAT by doing nothing themselves — they onboard as non-VAT-registered, set a USD Transfer Price excluding taxes/duties, and noon calculates, collects and pays destination VAT (and customs duties/clearance) and charges cross-border costs to the customer. Under the standard route, sellers handle it by obtaining a destination-country VAT Registration Certificate (UAE FTA / KSA ZATCA registration) and submitting it to noon; VAT-registered sellers "directly pay the VAT amounts to the government themselves" per the VAT FAQ.
- Caveat on authority: the NGS "no registration needed" position is **noon's own representation on its seller help pages**, not a ruling retrieved from the UAE FTA, Saudi ZATCA or Egypt ETA. No FTA/ZATCA/ETA page retrieved in this research addresses the NGS model specifically, so the tax-authority position on this exact structure is **UNKNOWN** from primary tax sources.

## Questions / sub-items that remain UNKNOWN, and why

1. **Q1 (partial): the complete qualification-document checklist for a Chinese NGS seller.** noon's China page names only the "valid Chinese Commercial Registration" / uploaded "Company Registration document," the "Non VAT Registered" selection, an authorized signatory and a valid payment method. Unlike the India Global page, it publishes no itemised document list (passport, bank proof, etc.). The full list appears to exist only inside the Seller Lab registration flow / account-manager onboarding (login-gated), which could not be retrieved.
2. **Q3 (partial): cross-border delivery time in days, from noon itself.** No noon page found states an NGS delivery-time figure. Secondary sources disagree (6–10 days vs 7–15 days) and are reported as such, not resolved.
3. **Q3 (partial): the cross-border shipping amount charged to the customer under NGS.** noon says such costs "will be charged to the customer directly" but publishes no amount or rate card for it.
4. **Q4 (partial): an explicit "settlement currency = USD" statement for Chinese (as opposed to Indian) Global sellers.** NGS pricing is set in USD and payout equals the USD Transfer Price, but the explicit settlement-currency sentence was found only on the India guide.
5. **Q5 (partial): the UAE FTA / Saudi ZATCA / Egypt ETA position on the NGS structure specifically.** noon's help pages assert sellers need not register and that noon pays destination VAT; no tax-authority page addressing this noon model was retrievable, and noon's own pages contradict each other for the non-NGS International Seller route (VAT FAQ "mandatory" vs. the disputed reading of the documents table).
6. **Egypt cross-border selling for a Chinese company: not UNKNOWN-but-unfound — affirmatively closed by the primary source** (documents page: company must be mandatorily registered in Egypt; Egypt absent from NGS destinations).

No page encountered attempted to redirect this research; no instructions embedded in sources were followed.
